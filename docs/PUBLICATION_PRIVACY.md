# Publication privacy

Public research should expose evidence and reproducible methods while minimizing personal identity information.

Before public promotion, inspect text, filenames, URLs, image metadata, document properties and archive members. Remove personal contact information, local account names, hostnames, credentials and private share links. Keep an exact original in the private archive and record the hash of each redacted derivative separately.

Use a pseudonym and GitHub's masked email for both author and committer metadata. Check every commit being published; a clean current file does not clean earlier commits, pull requests, forks, caches or copies already downloaded.

Keep raw transcripts, failed attempts, account exports and source catalogues in the private lab until reviewed. The public lab contains sanitized evidence with explicit reproduction steps and limits. Published article archives are historical sources, not automatic proof of their claims.

An owner-only Drive folder limits access, but Google still knows the account owner. Public handles, linked Medium articles and blockchain addresses can connect activity across services. These practices reduce exposure; they cannot guarantee anonymity.
